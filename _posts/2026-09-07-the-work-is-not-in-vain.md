If you read this blog, then you know that I evangelize proper documentation with
zeal more commonly associated with religious crusades<sup>1</sup>. I'm here to
tell you today that I have received a sign.

## Someone noticed

Apparently I have an academic fan? This was novel and unexpected, but at the 
closing beach bonfire for the [T2T F2F meeting][T2T], someone came up to me 
and went, "You're Faith! The one with all the good PRs!"

I don't think it's an exaggeration to say this was one of the proudest moments
of my life.<sup>2</sup>

Because yeah, I am the Faith with the good PRs. Or at least I try to be. I'm
the gal who makes [tiny quality-of-life PRs][QOLBlog] about (to pull a few
examples from tomorrow's release) "`vg snarls --traversals` related helptext
improved and useful errors added" or "`vg construct -S` will automatically turn
on `-f` (resolves #2932)"<sup>3</sup>. Things that, sure, don't _need_ to be
done, but that will make life smoother for the next person.

It meant a lot that someone noticed.

## Someone cared

And not only did this person _notice_ that I'd been polishing around the edges,
they explicitly voiced that it had been _useful_ for them. I [wrote][JoyBlog]
on this blog around a year ago:

> Improving usability is, far too often, nobody's job. Scientists have a rather
> bad habit of making code that works _for us_, writing a paper, releasing a link
> into the wild, and then... moving on to the next project.

I am perfectly aware that my usability updates won't get me more papers. That
was never the point. The point was to make the tool more _usable_. And here was
a user telling me unprompted (even more, having sought me out) that it I had, in
fact, made the tool more usable. That they had benefited from my work.

## It worked

And now for the real mother lode: not only did someone notice that I was trying,
and not only did they care, but they complimented the results. The following is
a paraphrase from memory:

> "You turned that **messy academic software** into something **professional**.
> I had been planning to work to be the only one in my lab who understood how to
> use `vg`, and then you came and took away my added value. Amazing."

And that independent confirmation that my goal of well-documented software had
at least gotten decently far along its way there? That was worth more than ten
papers, to me.<sup>4</sup>

So I'm here to tell you, my fellow workers in the documentation mud pits: the
work is not in vain. Keep at it. You're doing great.
